arena_lifetime_bench — 2026-09-03 — Linux x86_64 (4 cores), gcc -O2 (ccc build --release), ROUNDS=5 best-of
Branch: per-slab records with a lock-free bump. Compared against HEAD =
478db67 (the same lifetime model with the bump under the meta lock and
relaxed counters, receipt arena_lifetime_20260903_fastpaths.txt).

What changed:
- Each slab is its own record, created with its bytes and never rewritten.
  The tip and the live count are one 64-bit word (live:32 | offset:32).
  A bump, a tip pop, a last-live rewind, a tip regrow in place, and a
  checkpoint carve are each one CAS on that word; none takes the lock.
  Grow publishes a fresh record instead of moving the current one, so a
  bump that raced the grow still lands, and is credited, on the slab it
  read. The meta lock is left with grow, the overflow lists, the owner and
  class freelists, and the lifetime-parent records.
- A bump is an acquire CAS; a release, a tail pop, and a tip regrow are
  acq_rel, so the releasing thread's last accesses to the bytes happen
  before the bump that reuses them (the fully instrumented TSan build
  caught the acquire-only version reordering a read past its free).
- One slab holds at most 4 GiB (the offset half of the word); a larger
  request spills to overflow, and a larger L1 is refused at construction.
- Under REUSE, realloc rounds sizes the way alloc and release do, so a tip
  regrow in place cannot leave a block that a later sized release lists at
  its class size over the neighbour above it (a latent bug in the earlier
  tree; the adversarial test now has a deterministic case for it).

Notes:
- Contention is what this change buys: 2.5x on the contended bump and
  2.3x on contended alloc+release, with no lock holder to be preempted and
  no spin. It is still a single CAS word shared by every thread, so it
  bounces one cache line; glibc's per-thread tcache does not, which is the
  remaining gap on those two rows. A per-thread slab (each thread bumping
  its own record under the same host) would close it and is the natural
  next step on this design.
- Uncontended, the bump is one CAS either way, so the single-threaded rows
  are flat. The paths that now pay a CAS where a locked plain store used to
  do (checkpoint carve, tail pop, owner header + payload) lost 5 to 30 ns
  per cycle; the paths that dropped the lock entirely (tip regrow, tip pop,
  hole, buried realloc) gained about as much.
- String append at the tip regrows in place with one CAS and no lock:
  15 -> 5.4 ns on the 12-byte append row.
